# EXP-007: sustained ground escape through boost recovery

[CODEX_ENHANCEMENT_2026-11-21]

Target: official score **10.00+**; candidate performance remains unknown.

The current best boosts whenever requested with energy above 30. Once depleted
near that threshold, regeneration waits 0.6 seconds after spending, gains a small
amount, and then spending resumes. Frequent resets reduce the sustained boost
duty cycle. This is a code/mechanics diagnosis, not a diagnosis from match telemetry.

This candidate adds a recharge latch only for grounded, non-urgent decisions:
enter recovery at energy <=30, and resume boosts at 85% capacity. It does not
add boost requests, change directions or change combat. Urgent zone movement and
airborne jet decisions use the existing rule. All resource, weapon and upgrade
behavior is the 2.25 baseline, including 130 m weapon search. Neither retaliation,
kiting, V2 routing nor crossfire changes are included.

Hypothesis: longer boost bursts and fewer regen resets can create sustained
separation from pursuers and eventually permit health regeneration. Risk: walking
while recharging can expose the mech during an unfavorable engagement. The sole
official route to accept or reject this hypothesis is a new 48-match evaluation.

Local evidence: existing suite plus five boost tests passes. A deterministic
45-second energy-only accounting fixture uses default drain 17/s, regeneration
18/s and 0.6-second delay and verifies more boost time and fewer burst restarts.
It models no movement, projectiles or opponents and is not a game simulator.
The 300-observation A/B replay changes 54 boost flags and no other action fields.
Official checker: 300/300 in time, no late, stale or invalid actions; p50 3.3 ms,
p99 8.5 ms, maximum 9.1 ms locally. No survival, win-rate or placement claim is made.

Prior evidence: EXP-006 combining independently positive changes scored 2.19,
below 2.25. That candidate was not promoted. Broad V2 scored 1.31; its many changes
do not identify a cause. Standalone kiting scored 1.94 and crossfire 1.96. These
results argue for isolating this energy mechanism rather than stacking restrictions.
